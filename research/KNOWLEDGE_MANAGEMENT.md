# Knowledge Management

## Purpose

This document defines how knowledge is created, preserved, shared, and improved across all technology initiatives.

Knowledge is a long-term organizational asset.

---

# Core Principles

Knowledge should be:

- Accurate
- Accessible
- Documented
- Shareable
- Continuously improved

---

# Knowledge Sources

Knowledge may come from:

- Engineering experience
- Research
- Product development
- Testing
- User feedback
- Operational lessons

Every valuable lesson should be preserved.

---

# Documentation

Important knowledge should always be documented.

Examples include:

- Architecture decisions
- Design patterns
- Technical solutions
- Lessons learned
- Best practices

---

# Knowledge Sharing

Knowledge grows when it is shared.

Encourage:

- Documentation
- Technical discussions
- Design reviews
- Learning sessions

---

# Knowledge Preservation

Avoid knowledge loss by:

- Maintaining documentation
- Recording important decisions
- Updating technical references
- Preserving historical context

---

# Continuous Improvement

Knowledge should evolve with:

- New discoveries
- Better engineering practices
- Product improvements
- Industry developments

---

# Final Principle

Knowledge becomes valuable only when it can be understood, shared, and applied.
