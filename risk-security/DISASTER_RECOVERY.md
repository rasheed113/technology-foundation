# Disaster Recovery

## Purpose

This document defines the principles and strategies for recovering critical systems, services, and data following major disruptions or disasters.

Disaster recovery ensures that the organization can restore operations efficiently while protecting users, data, and long-term business continuity.

---

# Disaster Recovery Philosophy

Disasters cannot always be prevented.

Prepared organizations minimize their impact through planning, resilience, regular testing, and disciplined recovery procedures.

Recovery planning is an investment in long-term stability.

---

# Objectives

Disaster recovery aims to:

- Restore critical services
- Recover essential data
- Minimize downtime
- Protect organizational assets
- Reduce operational disruption
- Enable safe and orderly recovery

Recovery efforts should prioritize user trust and service reliability.

---

# Disaster Scenarios

Recovery planning should consider events such as:

- Infrastructure failures
- Hardware failures
- Software failures
- Cybersecurity incidents
- Data corruption
- Natural disasters
- Human error
- Third-party service outages

Planning should account for both expected and unexpected events.

---

# Recovery Strategy

Recovery strategies should include:

- Verified backups
- Recovery procedures
- Infrastructure restoration
- Data validation
- Service verification
- Controlled return to normal operations

Recovery plans should be documented and regularly updated.

---

# Recovery Priorities

Recovery activities should prioritize:

1. Protection of people
2. Protection of critical data
3. Restoration of essential infrastructure
4. Recovery of core services
5. Verification of system integrity
6. Full operational restoration

Recovery should be systematic rather than rushed.

---

# Testing

Disaster recovery procedures should be tested periodically through:

- Backup restoration tests
- Recovery simulations
- Infrastructure recovery exercises
- Documentation reviews

Testing ensures that recovery plans remain practical and effective.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Recovery capabilities should improve through:

- Incident analysis
- Engineering feedback
- Recovery testing
- Lessons learned
- Technology improvements

Preparedness is strengthened through continuous learning.

---

# Final Principle

Disaster recovery is successful when critical services, data, and operations can be restored safely, efficiently, and with confidence.

Preparation before a disaster is the foundation of successful recovery.
